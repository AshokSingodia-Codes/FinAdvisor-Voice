### 3. Build a Diversified Portfolio
Diversification reduces the impact of any single investment’s poor performance.

| Asset Class | Typical Role | Example Vehicles |
|-------------|--------------|------------------|
| **Cash & Cash Equivalents** | Liquidity, capital preservation | Money‑market funds, high‑yield savings accounts |
| **Fixed Income** | Income, lower volatility | Treasury bonds, municipal bonds, corporate bond ETFs |

| **Real Assets** | Inflation hedge, diversification | REITs, commodities, infrastructure funds |
| **Alternative/Private** | Potentially uncorrelated returns | Hedge funds, private equity, venture capital (usually for accredited investors) |

- **Tax efficiency:** Prefer tax‑advantaged accounts (e.g., 401(k), IRA, Roth IRA) for long‑term growth; consider municipal bonds for taxable income.

## User Query: What is the difference between Direct and Indirect Taxation?
**Routing Decision**: `direct_answer`

**Draft Answer**:
**Direct vs. Indirect Taxation**

| Aspect | Direct Taxation | Indirect Taxation |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Definition** | Taxes levied directly on individuals or entities based on their income, wealth, or property. The taxpayer cannot shift the burden to someone else. | Taxes levied on goods and services that are collected by an intermediary (e.g., retailer, manufacturer) and passed on to the government. The economic burden can be shifted to the final consumer. |
| **Typical Examples** | • Income tax<br>• Corporate tax<br>• Capital gains tax<br>• Property tax<br>• Wealth tax | • Value‑Added Tax (VAT) / Goods and Services Tax (GST)<br>• Sales tax<br>• Excise duties (e.g., on alcohol, tobacco, fuel)<br>• Customs duties |
| **Who Pays Directly to the Government?** | The taxpayer (individual or corporation) pays directly to the tax authority. | The intermediary (seller, producer) collects the tax from the buyer and remits it to the tax authority. |
| **Incidence (Who Bears the Economic Burden?)** | The burden stays with the person or entity on whom the tax is imposed. | The burden can be passed on to downstream parties, usually the final consumer, through higher prices. |
| **Progressivity** | Often progressive (higher rates for higher income/wealth), aiming at equity. | Generally regressive, because the tax takes a larger proportion of income from lower‑income consumers (they spend a higher share of their income on taxed goods). |
| **Visibility to Taxpayer** | Highly visible; taxpayers see the amount owed on tax returns, pay‑stubs, etc. | Less visible; the tax is embedded in the price of goods/services, so consumers may not notice the separate tax component. |
| **Administration** | Requires filing of returns, assessments, audits, and often complex compliance for individuals and corporations. | Collected at the point of sale; compliance is mainly on businesses (registration, invoicing, periodic filing). |
| **Impact on Economic Decisions** | Influences decisions on work, investment, savings, and corporate structure. | Influences consumption choices, pricing strategies, and supply‑chain decisions. |

### Key Takeaway
- **Direct taxes** are imposed directly on the income or wealth of the taxpayer and cannot be shifted to others.
- **Indirect taxes** are levied on transactions (goods/services) and are typically passed on to the end consumer through higher prices.
